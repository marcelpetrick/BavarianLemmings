# BavarianLemmings: vision and specification

> Living document. Phase: **requirements engineering** (no code yet).
> Owner: Marcel Petrick. Last update: 2026-10-11 (rounds A to C answered).

## 1. Vision

BavarianLemmings is a faithful re-creation of **Lemmings** (DMA Design / Psygnosis, 1991) as it
looked, sounded and played on a **386 PC under DOS**. It runs natively in the browser (Firefox and
Chrome), built with web technology. It does not emulate, recompile or wrap the original binary.

Lemmings drop out of an entrance hatch and walk on without stopping. The player gives single
lemmings jobs (dig, block, build, bash, explode, and so on) that change the landscape, so that enough
of them reach the exit door before the time runs out.

The game has **two modes**:

1. **Classic mode** (the default): the look, sound, lemming types, skills and level design of the
   DOS original, as close as we can get without copying any of its material.
2. **Bavarian mode**, switched on in the settings: the same game logic, re-themed as Bavaria. The
   colour scheme becomes the white and blue of the Bavarian flag, the backgrounds become Alpine
   mountains, and the music changes to Alpine folk music. Everything that can be themed is themed.

The project is an **homage**: a fond memory of a funny game from the early 90s. It does not infringe
copyright. No original graphics, sounds, music or level files are used or shipped.

## 2. Guiding principles

- **The DOS original is the reference.** Amiga, console and later ports (Lemmings 2, Oh No! More
  Lemmings, remakes) are not the reference. If a port differs from DOS, DOS wins.
- **Homage, not a copy.** Every asset is either generated by us (pixel art, procedural or
  synthesised audio) or comes from a source with a compatible free licence (CC0, CC-BY or
  CC-BY-SA, or a GPL-compatible licence), recorded in `THIRD_PARTY.md`. We copy the mechanics, the
  feel and the style, never the material.
- **2D only.** Flat pixel graphics, as in the original. No 3D, no pseudo-3D.
- **Native web, no emulation.** No DOSBox/js-dos, no emulator, no WebAssembly build of the original
  executable.
- **Understandable by humans.** Every module, script and decision is documented and reviewed. Code is
  written to be read.
- **Verify facts before stating them.** Original-game details marked `[verify]` below are unconfirmed
  and must be checked (original manual, disassembly-based community documentation, a real DOS run)
  before they become a requirement.

#### 3.4.1 Touch proposal (for review, Q-D1 to Q-D3)

The problem: the original is designed for a mouse. A lemming is about 6 × 10 logical pixels. There is
no hover on touch (the original's selection box only appears under the cursor). And the bottom panel
of 320 × 40 pixels has buttons that are much smaller than a finger.

The proposal:

1. **One input layer for everything.** Pointer Events (mouse, pen, touch) go through one input
   module. The game logic never sees whether a click came from a mouse or a finger.
2. **Two UI layouts, one playfield.** The playfield is always the same pixel-exact 320 × 160 logical
   image, scaled by an integer factor. Only the **controls** change:
   - *Classic layout:* the original 320 × 40 panel underneath, scaled together with the playfield.
   - *Touch layout:* the same buttons, but drawn independently with a minimum target size of
     48 × 48 CSS pixels (WCAG 2.5.5 / Material guidelines). In **landscape**, the skill buttons sit
     in two columns to the left and right of the playfield (thumbs reach them), and the minimap and
     the status line sit above. In **portrait**, the playfield sits at the top at full width and a large
     two-row panel sits underneath.
   - *Auto* (the default) picks the touch layout when the primary pointer is coarse
     (`@media (pointer: coarse)`). It can be overridden in the settings at any time.
3. **Assigning a skill by touch: "press, aim, release".** Touching the playfield shows the selection
   box on the best lemming within a radius of about 8 mm around the finger (the original's priority
   rules decide). Sliding the finger moves the box. **Lifting the finger assigns the skill.** Sliding
   out of the playfield cancels. A small magnifier above the finger (2×) shows what is under it, like
   the text magnifier in mobile operating systems.
4. **Scrolling and zoom.** Dragging with two fingers, or dragging on the minimap, scrolls the view.
   Pinching zooms (a modern convenience, C-05). One finger is reserved for assigning, so scrolling
   never assigns a skill by accident.
5. **Panel buttons.** Release rate −/+ repeat while held. **Nuke** needs a press of one second with
   a progress ring (or a double tap), so it cannot be triggered by accident. Pause, fast-forward and
   restart are big buttons.
6. **The browser stays out of the way.** `touch-action: none` on the game area, no pull-to-refresh,
   no double-tap zoom, no text selection, no context menu on long press. A fullscreen button. The game
   pauses automatically when the page is hidden (another app, a locked screen).
7. **Testing.** Playwright with touch emulation (`hasTouch`, tablet viewports in both orientations)
   in CI, plus a manual check on a real tablet before each release.

### 3.5 Audio (A)

| A-05 | MUST: classic mode music: our own arrangements, with AdLib-like FM sound, of the **public-domain melodies** used by the original, plus our own compositions. Only melodies that are demonstrably in the public domain are used. Every arrangement records the source of its melody in `THIRD_PARTY.md` (Q-C5). |
